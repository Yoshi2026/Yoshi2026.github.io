Featured Project

MEXT SPReAD 2026

WTO Dispute Settlement in Practice: Uncovering Its Actual Use through AI

Research question

WTO dispute settlement is usually studied through legal texts, institutional design and individual disputes. This project asks a different empirical question: What does WTO dispute settlement actually look like when its operation is examined systematically through data?

By combining AI with large-scale data, the project seeks to make patterns in the use of WTO dispute settlement visible that are difficult to observe through individual case studies alone.

Why AI?

WTO dispute settlement has generated a large body of legal documents and related information that is difficult to process systematically by human researchers alone. This project uses AI as a research instrument for turning large bodies of legal information into systematically analysable evidence, rather than simply as a tool for generating text.

The project is a concrete application of the broader methodological direction: making hidden realities visible through law × AI × data.

Broader significance

The project aims to contribute to a more evidence-based understanding of international economic law by connecting legal analysis with AI-assisted empirical research.

Economic coercion · Trade diversion · 2026

Does Economic Coercion Work?

Evidence from China’s Import Restrictions on Japanese Seafood. The analysis tracks monthly export values for 213 nine-digit Japanese seafood product categories from January 2022 through July 2026. It examines whether products that had depended more heavily on China before the restriction subsequently increased exports to third-country markets.

Key result: a 10 percentage point higher pre-restriction China export share was associated with an estimated 16.9% higher monthly export value to the rest of the world after the restrictions.

Trade × Sustainability · Current project

ACCTS — Environmental Goods & Fossil-Fuel Subsidies

This project uses trade and fossil-fuel-support data to examine the gap between legal innovation and actual policy change under the Agreement on Climate Change, Trade and Sustainability (ACCTS).

The environmental-goods analysis maps the final ACCTS Annex II list to 316 unique HS6 subheadings and compares pre-ACCTS tariff treatment across 66 economies using UNCTAD TRAINS / World Bank WITS tariff data, including preferential treatment under FTAs and other arrangements. The fossil-fuel-subsidy analysis combines OECD Fossil Fuel Support data with measure-level legal analysis to examine how existing support policies interact with the new ACCTS disciplines.

The manuscript has been submitted for publication. The paper and supplementary research materials are not publicly available at this stage. Following publication, and subject to the publisher’s policies, the underlying datasets, methodological documentation and replication materials are intended to be released here.

Research design & data — details

Research question

ACCTS creates legally novel disciplines, but legal novelty does not necessarily imply large immediate changes in the policies of its founding signatories. The project asks how much Costa Rica, Iceland, New Zealand and Switzerland would actually need to change their existing policies to comply with the Agreement’s rules on environmental goods and fossil-fuel subsidies.

Environmental goods

ACCTS Annex II contains 360 entries. After removing repeated HS6 codes, the empirical analysis works with 316 unique HS6 subheadings. It uses effectively applied tariff (AHS) data from UNCTAD TRAINS / World Bank WITS to identify pre-ACCTS tariff treatment, including preferential rates available under FTAs and other arrangements.

The four ACCTS signatories are compared with a broader sample of 66 economies. This distinguishes the creation of a new legal obligation from the amount of additional tariff liberalisation that the founding members actually need to undertake.

Fossil-fuel subsidies

This part uses OECD Fossil Fuel Support data to compare recorded support across the four ACCTS signatories. It then conducts measure-level legal analysis of major Swiss support measures. The analysis distinguishes measures recorded as support in the OECD dataset from the separate legal question of whether those measures fall within the definitions, prohibitions and exceptions in ACCTS Chapter 4.

Replication materials

Three supplementary research workbooks have been prepared for independent verification and replication:

  • Mapping of ACCTS Annex II Entries to Unique HS6 Subheadings
  • AHS-Based Tariff Analysis of ACCTS Annex II Environmental Goods
  • OECD Fossil Fuel Support Comparison

These materials are not publicly available while the manuscript is in the Springer publication process. They are planned for release following publication, subject to the publisher’s policies.

About

Law in context.

I am an Associate Professor at the School of International Relations, University of Shizuoka. My research focuses on evidence-based analysis of international trade regulation, with particular interest in risk, sustainability, public health, geoeconomics, and the use of data to examine how international economic law operates in practice.

Before entering academia, I served in Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs, working on WTO dispute settlement. I received my PhD in Law from the University of Bern, Switzerland, where I completed my dissertation, International Trade in Food after a Nuclear Accident, with the distinction of magna cum laude. I currently serve as a Board Member of the Japanese Society of International Economic Law.
